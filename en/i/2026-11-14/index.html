NYK sets the exterior design for its new Marunouchi headquarters in Tokyo, with a ship's wake on the ground floor

Shipping company NYK Line has settled the outline and exterior design of the building that will replace its Yusen Building head office in Tokyo's Marunouchi district. A London firm is designing the exterior, and completion is planned for 2033.

NYK Line (Nippon Yusen), one of Japan's largest shipping companies, announced on November 13 that it has decided the outline and exterior design of its new head office, which will replace the Yusen Building, its current head office in Marunouchi, the business district next to Tokyo Station. Completion is planned for 2033. Mitsubishi Jisho Design is the architect for the building as a whole, and the exterior is being designed by PLP Architecture, a firm based in London.

Fitting for a shipping company, the exterior draws on ships and waves. On the ground floor, a wall that folds as it runs along the street is meant to evoke the wake a ship leaves behind, a motif the company calls SHIP WAKE. The plan also adds pilotis, open-air space under the building where it is lifted on columns, along all three streets the site faces: Hibiya-dori, Gyoko-dori and Marunouchi Naka-dori. The aim is to connect the building to the flow of pedestrians around it.

The Yusen Building stands on the corner of Gyoko-dori, the avenue that runs between the Imperial Palace and Tokyo Station. NYK first said it was considering a rebuild in July 2023, when it aimed for completion in the early 2030s. This announcement puts a specific year on it.

Briefing
  • According to NYK's announcement, Mitsubishi Jisho Design is the architect for the whole building and the UK firm PLP Architecture is handling the exterior design. The project outline in the official announcement lists Toda Corporation as the planned contractor.
  • When the rebuild plan was first reported in July 2023, NYK said that during construction it would move its head office functions to a new building it is constructing in Yokohama, the port city just south of Tokyo.
  • The current Yusen Building was completed in 1978 and houses NYK's head office. The announcement also names linking the street-side pilotis to the surrounding pedestrian network as one of the plan's core ideas.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

The ground-floor wall keeps folding as it runs along the street. Think of the white V-shaped waves a boat leaves on the water behind it. The wall traces that shape. Office towers in Marunouchi tend to look alike on their upper floors. So if a building wants its own character, the place to win is the lower floors, close to the eye level of people walking by. This plan commits to pilotis along three streets. A piloti is when you lift a building on columns so people can pass underneath. Walk under an elevated train line and you feel it: there's a roof over you, but you're still outside, right? I think this will feel close to that. Gyoko-dori runs straight from Tokyo Station to the Imperial Palace, one of the most formal streets in Tokyo. On that corner, the wake-shaped wall will be what greets people. Completion is set for 2033, seven years from now.

Background
Marunouchi's rebuild cycle

In Marunouchi, buildings from around the 1970s are reaching the age for rebuilding one after another. In new towers there, opening up the lower floors to the city as plazas or walk-through passages is becoming the standard approach. The Yusen Building plan follows that trend.

A foreign firm for the facade

For large buildings in Japan, it is fairly common for a Japanese firm to design the building as a whole while a foreign firm designs only the exterior. The Japanese side handles coordination with codes, structure and construction, and the exterior, the face the building shows the city, is where the distinct character comes from.

Sumitomo Life plans a 20-story mixed-use tower on the former Century Royal Hotel site in Sapporo

On the site of the former Century Royal Hotel near Sapporo Station, Sumitomo Life Insurance is planning a 20-story mixed-use building with shops, offices and a hotel of about 300 rooms. It aims to start construction in April 2028.

Sumitomo Life Insurance is planning a new mixed-use building on the site of the former Century Royal Hotel, which closed in 2024, at Kita 5-jo Nishi 5-chome in Sapporo's Chuo Ward, it emerged at a meeting of the city's urban planning council. Sapporo is the largest city on Hokkaido, Japan's northernmost main island. The building will have one basement level and 20 floors above ground, with total floor area of about 49,500 m² (533,000 sq ft). Construction is targeted to start in April 2028. Several shops will fill the basement and ground floor, and the second floor and above will be used for offices and a hotel of about 300 rooms.

The city of Sapporo plans to revise the zoning for the block and relax its limits on floor area ratio (FAR), the total floor space allowed relative to the size of the lot, and on height. Zone A, which includes the former hotel site, will get a FAR of 1,181%, and the neighboring Zone B 878%, while the height limit will rise from a maximum of 82 m (269 ft) to 100 m (328 ft). Demolition of the old hotel building is nearly finished.

Briefing
  • The Century Royal Hotel closed on May 31, 2024, because its lease with Sumitomo Life, which owns the building, was expiring and the building was aging. Known for having the only revolving observation restaurant in Hokkaido, it ended a 51-year history.
  • Under the zoning change, the former hotel site becomes Zone A and the neighboring lot Zone B, each with its own FAR cap. The height limit will be raised from a maximum of 82 m (269 ft) to 100 m (328 ft).
  • The plan disclosed this time does not include the hotel operator or an opening date. The April 2028 construction start is still only a target, and the plan will firm up after further zoning procedures.

Walk out of Sapporo Station's south exit and a little to the west, and you reach a corner. A hotel stood there with a restaurant at the top that slowly turned. That was the Century Royal Hotel, which closed in May 2024. Now a plan for a 20-story mixed-use building on that site is coming into view. The key this time is the floor area ratio. FAR is the cap on how many times the lot's area you're allowed to build as floor space. Say you have a 100-tsubo lot (a tsubo is about 3.3 m² (35.6 sq ft)) with a FAR of 1,000%: you can build up to 1,000 tsubo of floor in total. Sapporo plans to raise the cap for this block and allow heights up to 100 m (328 ft). With construction costs rising, the only way to make a rebuild work is to add floor space and build up the rental income. If shops line the ground floor, I think the view from the sidewalk in front of the station will change quite a bit. If you're ever in Sapporo, next time you walk out of the station's south exit, go see for yourself how big the empty lot is on the corner of Kita 5-jo Nishi 5-chome.

Background
Sapporo's station-area rebuilds

Around Sapporo Station, major redevelopments and rebuilds have kept coming, with an eye on the planned extension of the Hokkaido Shinkansen bullet train. Meanwhile, the redevelopment of the Kita 5 Nishi 1 and Nishi 2 district on the station's south side is revising its plan, including dropping a hotel. Sumitomo Life's plan is moving forward in that setting as a single private building.

Bonuses come with conditions

Extra floor area is generally granted in exchange for providing spaces or functions that serve the area. Improving pedestrian space or bringing in uses the city needs, such as hotels, is often cited as the basis for relaxing the limits.

Asahi Kasei Homes launches a tender offer of up to about ¥12.8 billion to take over general contractor Mori-Gumi

Asahi Kasei Homes, the house builder behind the Hebel Haus brand, has launched a tender offer aiming to make Osaka general contractor Mori-Gumi a wholly owned subsidiary. The goal is to share construction staff and subcontractor networks between the two.

Asahi Kasei Homes, the house-building subsidiary of chemicals group Asahi Kasei, announced on November 12 that it will make general contractor Mori-Gumi a wholly owned subsidiary through a tender offer, a public offer to buy shares from all shareholders. The offer price is ¥564 per share, for a total of up to about ¥12.8 billion. The offer runs from November 13 to January 7, 2027.

Asahi Kasei Homes is already Mori-Gumi's largest shareholder, with about 30% of its shares. Mori-Gumi supports the offer and recommends that its shareholders tender. Haseko Corporation, the second-largest shareholder, has also agreed to tender all of its shares. If the offer succeeds, Mori-Gumi is expected to be delisted.

As for the aim, the companies cite making mutual use of their construction staff and their networks of subcontractors. With labor shortages persisting across the construction industry, they want to strengthen their building capacity, shorten construction periods and become more cost-competitive. They also expect to win more orders by drawing on the Asahi Kasei group's customer base and sales network.

Briefing
  • The offer price of ¥564 is a premium of 79.05% over Mori-Gumi's closing price of ¥315 on November 11, the last trading day before the announcement. The minimum number of shares to be purchased is 11,922,100, and there is no maximum.
  • Haseko Corporation, the second-largest shareholder, holds 8.01% of Mori-Gumi's shares. Asahi Kasei disclosed that it has signed an agreement with Haseko under which Haseko will tender all of its shares.
  • After the announcement, the Tokyo Stock Exchange designated Mori-Gumi as a security under supervision (under confirmation) on November 12. On November 13, Mori-Gumi's share price moved toward the tender offer price.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

Do you think of the company that builds your house and the company that builds office towers as two different things? Actually, the line between them is slowly fading. Here, house builder Asahi Kasei Homes is trying to bring Mori-Gumi, a general contractor that builds condominiums and roads, fully into its group. What construction sites are shortest on right now isn't money. It's people. Even with drawings in hand, if you can't line up the engineers who run the site and the tradespeople who tie rebar and build formwork, a building won't rise a single millimeter. A subcontractor network is, in a sense, a list of tradespeople who will come with a single phone call when you need them. If two companies can share that list, then when one side's sites get busy, the other can send people over. The offer price is about 80% above the share price just before the announcement. I think what they want badly enough to pay that is the ability to reach tradespeople. In an era when how many builders you can keep on hand sets a company's price, who do you think moves next?

Background
House builders buying build capacity

With new housing starts sluggish, Japan's house builders are expanding into mid- and high-rise condominiums and non-residential buildings. That requires the capacity to run sites on a different scale from detached houses. Bringing a general contractor into the group is one shortcut to it.

Labor shortage and consolidation

Construction workers in Japan are aging, and too few young people are entering the trade. The government-wide task force on construction labor, as we reported on Nov 9, comes out of the same shortage. To secure workers, consolidation through mergers is spreading to mid-sized and smaller construction companies too.
