Togo Murano's former Yokohama City Hall, now the OMO7 Yokohama hotel, wins a Good Design Gold Award

The 2026 Good Design Award winners were announced on October 15, and OMO7 Yokohama, a hotel created inside the administrative wing of Yokohama's former city hall, completed in 1959, received a Gold Award.

The Japan Institute of Design Promotion announced the winners of the 2026 Good Design Award, Japan's major annual design award, on October 15. Of 5,111 entries reviewed, 1,484 received the award. Among the 19 Gold Awards, chosen from the top entries in each judging unit, was OMO7 Yokohama by Hoshino Resorts, a hotel in Kannai, the historic downtown of the port city of Yokohama just south of Tokyo. The top prize, the Good Design Grand Award, went to Yamaha's EAD50 drum expansion module.

OMO7 Yokohama occupies the administrative wing of the former Yokohama City Hall, designed by architect Togo Murano and completed in 1959. Instead of being torn down, the building was converted into a hotel, which opened on April 21, 2026. It is part of BASEGATE Yokohama Kannai, the redevelopment of the old city hall block in front of JR Kannai Station. The judges singled out the originality of letting guests stay inside a piece of postwar modernist architecture as it is.

According to operator Hoshino Resorts, this is the first Gold Award for its OMO brand of city hotels. Takenaka Corporation handled the renovation design, and Naruse Inokuma Architects designed and supervised the interiors around the guest rooms. Tokyu, a partner in the project, and Iris Ohyama, which did the detailed design of the built-in furniture, have each announced the award as well.

Briefing
  • The Good Design Award's special prizes total 32: one Grand Award, 19 Gold Awards and 12 Good Focus Awards. The Good Design Best 100, made up of the top five entries in each judging unit, was selected at the same time.
  • Winning entries will be shown at the award exhibition, “G-ten,” opening October 31 at Tokyo Midtown, a mixed-use complex in central Tokyo.
  • OMO7 Yokohama has 276 guest rooms. According to Hoshino Resorts, earlier OMO hotels recognized by the program include OMO5 Tokyo Otsuka, which made the Good Design Best 100, and OMO5 Okinawa Naha and OMO5 Kumamoto, which received the Good Design Award.
  • The administrative wing of the former Yokohama City Hall was built as part of the events marking the 100th anniversary of the opening of Yokohama's port. It is a reinforced concrete building with eight floors above ground and one basement level. In the 2009 seismic upgrade, seismic isolation devices were built into the existing foundation, preserving both the exterior and the interior.

Out of 5,111 entries, just 19 Gold Awards. And one of them went to a building put up as a city hall 67 years ago. Think of it like a favorite chair: you leave the chair alone and just put new cushions under its legs. My view is that this groundwork is what kept conversion on the table instead of demolition and rebuilding. In 2025 it became the first postwar building to be named a Certified Historic Building by the City of Yokohama, and this April it opened as a hotel. Falling asleep by the window of what used to be city hall is a slightly strange experience, don't you think? When you keep an old building, things a new building could decide freely, like ceiling heights and window positions, are already fixed. Even so, a building someone judged worth keeping now sits on the top tier of a design award. I think we are slowly moving into an era where the decision to keep a building gets recognized.

Background
Saving postwar buildings

Compared with Western-style buildings from the Meiji and Taisho eras (the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries), Japan's postwar modernist buildings have often been seen as “still too new” to protect, and many have been replaced because of aging or poor earthquake resistance. In August 2025, the administrative wing of the former Yokohama City Hall became the first postwar structure to be named a Certified Historic Building by the City of Yokohama. Converting such buildings to new uses is spreading as a practical way to keep them.

Seismic isolation retrofit

A way of upgrading an existing building's earthquake performance by, in effect, lifting it and adding seismic isolation devices such as laminated rubber bearings at the foundation. Because it avoids adding braces or extra walls to the columns and walls, it is often chosen for historic buildings whose design needs to be preserved.

Nasushiobara puts its new city hall out to bid a third time, raising the ceiling price to ¥15.8 billion

After two failed bids, Nasushiobara, a city in Tochigi Prefecture north of Tokyo, has issued a third call for bids on its new city hall. The ceiling price is up by about ¥2.6 billion, and bids will be opened in January.

The city of Nasushiobara in Tochigi Prefecture issued a new call for open competitive bids on October 14 for construction of its new city hall. Bidding in August 2025 and again in March 2026 failed because the bidders withdrew, so this is the third attempt. The ceiling price, the most the city is willing to pay, is ¥15.84 billion before tax. Applications to bid will be accepted until November 20, and bids are scheduled to be opened on January 13, 2027.

In revising the terms, the city raised the ceiling price by about ¥2.6 billion and relaxed some of the eligibility rules. Bidders must be special-purpose joint ventures of three to five companies. The minimum overall score the lead company needs on the keishin, Japan's national rating of contractors' size, finances and technical capacity for public works, has been lowered from 1,700 points to 1,400. The city also dropped the scoring method that weighed technical proposals and switched to selecting the winner on price alone.

Facing rising material and labor costs, the city has increased the construction budget for the new city hall to ¥17.5 billion. One of its funding sources, merger special bonds, can only be issued until the end of fiscal 2029, and the city aims to finish the building by then. Japan's fiscal year runs from April to March.

Briefing
  • The new city hall is one of the projects in the redevelopment around JR Nasushiobara Station, with a total floor area of about 17,000 m² (183,000 sq ft). The building, its mechanical and electrical systems and the site work will be let as a single contract.
  • The joint venture must include at least two members with a head office or branch in the city. Starting this round, companies based in Tochigi Prefecture with a keishin score of 1,000 points or more can also join as members.
  • Along with the higher construction budget, the multi-year appropriation was reworked: ¥7 billion in fiscal 2027, ¥7 billion in fiscal 2028 and ¥3.5 billion in fiscal 2029.
  • The ceiling price was ¥11.2 billion in the first round and ¥13.3 billion in the second (both figures as reported), so the current ¥15.8 billion works out to about 40% more than the first round.

Background
Failed bids keep piling up

As material prices and labor costs rise, the ceiling prices estimated by local governments often no longer cover contractors' costs, and no one bids. These failed tenders keep happening on large public projects such as city halls and gymnasiums across Japan. As we reported on Oct 13, the new city hall in Kishiwada, a city in Osaka Prefecture, switched to separate contracts after its design-build tender failed.

The merger bond deadline

Merger special bonds are a form of favorable borrowing available to municipalities created in Japan's great wave of municipal mergers during the Heisei era, with much of the repayment covered by the national government's local allocation tax grants. Once the deadline passes, the bonds can no longer be issued, so many towns rush to rebuild their city halls before it. That also leaves less slack in construction schedules.

New terminal opens at Hollywood Burbank Airport near Los Angeles, a $1.3 billion project

A new terminal designed by Corgan opened on October 13 at Hollywood Burbank Airport in California. It has 14 gates and is billed as an all-electric building.

A new passenger terminal opened on October 13 at Hollywood Burbank Airport in Burbank, California. The building opened its doors at 5 a.m., and the first flight from the new terminal departed at 7 a.m. With 14 gates, it replaces the existing terminal.

U.S. firm Corgan designed the terminal together with CannonDesign and Burns & McDonnell. It has a total floor area of about 33,000 m² (355,000 sq ft) and is described as an all-electric terminal, meaning its building systems run on electricity. According to local TV station KTLA, the project cost $1.3 billion, and the terminal is expected to serve 6 million to 7 million passengers a year.

The design draws on the atmosphere of classic Hollywood. The entrance canopy is lined with columns inspired by the searchlights that sweep the night sky at movie premieres. At the same time, the terminal has no jet bridges: passengers will keep walking outdoors to board and leave their planes, as they did before. The old terminal will be demolished.

Briefing
  • The design team also included Holder, Pankow and TEC, the joint venture (HPTJV) building the terminal.
  • With the new terminal, the airport entrance moved, and the intersection of Winona Avenue and Hollywood Way became the new front door.
  • Most reports put the total floor area at 355,000 sq ft, while some, including ABC7, describe it as “more than 300,000 sq ft,” so the figures vary.

Background
An airport without jet bridges

Jet bridges protect passengers from rain and cold, but they cost money to build and maintain. Hollywood Burbank Airport's old terminal already had passengers walk outdoors to and from their planes, and the new terminal keeps that style. Whether to install jet bridges depends on a terminal's size and climate.

All-electric buildings

An all-electric building runs its heating, cooling and hot water on electricity rather than burning gas or other fuels. Paired with renewable power, it is seen as a way to cut a building's carbon dioxide emissions during operation. For a large facility like an airport, with millions of people passing through each year, the choice of energy directly affects both operating costs and environmental impact.
