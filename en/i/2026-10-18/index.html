Demolition of Kenzo Tange's "ship gymnasium" resumes as Kagawa assembly rejects plea to pause

Demolition of the former Kagawa Prefectural Gymnasium, halted for a month during a residents' lawsuit, has resumed. On Oct 16 the prefectural assembly rejected a petition asking that work wait for an expert seismic assessment.

Demolition of the former Kagawa Prefectural Gymnasium in Takamatsu, designed by Kenzo Tange and completed in 1964, resumed on October 15. Takamatsu is the capital of Kagawa Prefecture on the island of Shikoku. Work had been suspended from September 15 to October 14 after the Takamatsu District Court proposed a pause in a residents' lawsuit, the legal route in Japan for residents to challenge a local government's spending. During the pause, the plaintiffs carried out an on-site survey of the building's seismic performance and other conditions, finishing on the 13th.

A preservation group, the Former Kagawa Prefectural Gymnasium Revitalization Committee, had petitioned the prefectural assembly to stop the work until results came in from a seismic assessment by an expert chosen by the court. On October 16, the Kagawa Prefectural Assembly rejected the petition. The assessment is expected to take two to three months, and the prefecture plans to go ahead with the work without waiting for the results.

The gymnasium uses a suspended roof structure that borrows bridge-building techniques to create a large, column-free space. Its shape, like a traditional Japanese wooden boat, earned it the nickname "the ship gymnasium." It closed in September 2014 over concerns that its aging ceiling could fall, and the prefecture decided in 2023 to demolish it.

Briefing
  • Demolition work began on April 10, 2026. In November 2025, the Revitalization Committee filed the residents' lawsuit seeking to stop public money from being spent on the demolition.
  • Separately from the residents' lawsuit, people living nearby have also applied for a provisional injunction to halt the demolition.
  • The Revitalization Committee has presented the prefecture with a plan to seismically retrofit the building and use it as a hotel or similar facility, and says the cost of about ¥3 billion to ¥6 billion could be covered entirely by private money. Kenzo Tange's eldest son, Noritaka Tange, had also issued a statement calling for the building's preservation.

Background
Same year as Yoyogi

1964, the year the gymnasium was completed, was also the year Tange's Yoyogi National Gymnasium in Tokyo was completed. The Kagawa gymnasium is sometimes described as a kind of prototype for it, and it used what was then cutting-edge technology for spanning large spaces with a suspended roof.

Its role passes to SANAA

The prefectural gym's functions have moved to Anabuki Arena Kagawa, which opened on February 24, 2025. It was designed by SANAA (Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa), cost about ¥20.2 billion, and its main arena holds about 10,000 people. When it comes to preserving landmark buildings, how to keep using a building that has lost its original purpose is a challenge shared by many places.

Developers to renovate an entire 14-story building in Kobe into 161 condos instead of rebuilding

Sankei Building and two other companies will renovate a whole 14-story building in Kobe's Higashinada Ward into 161 condominiums for sale, keeping the existing structure. Move-in is planned for March 2028.

Sankei Building, Sogo Jisho and Marubeni Toshi Kaihatsu announced on October 16 that they will develop condominiums for sale through a whole-building renovation in Kitaogi 2-chome, Higashinada Ward, Kobe, the port city west of Osaka. The project's working name is the Kobe Motoyama GO BEYOND Mansion Project (in Japan, "mansion" means a condominium building). Haseko Corporation also announced the project the same day.

The building has 14 stories above ground and 161 units in total. The existing structural frame (the concrete skeleton) will be kept, and the interiors of all 161 units will be completely renovated. Layouts range from 2LDK+S to 4LDK (two bedrooms with a living-dining-kitchen and a storage room, up to four bedrooms with a living-dining-kitchen), with unit floor areas of 66.51 to 90.40 m² (720–970 sq ft).

The developers plan to obtain ZEH-M Oriented certification, a Japanese energy-efficiency rating for apartment buildings under the country's net zero energy house program. Both completion of the renovation and move-in are scheduled for late March 2028. The project's official website went live on October 15.

Briefing
  • The nearest station is Ogi Station on the Hanshin Main Line, an 8-minute walk away. Settsu-Motoyama Station on the JR Kobe Line is an 18-minute walk.
  • Some units are planned to have a "Kitchen Atelier Plan" built around the kitchen, and a vanity unit with an integrated stand for Kanta-kun, a gas-powered clothes dryer.
  • Prices and the start of sales could not be confirmed from the announcement materials.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

How do you judge how old a condo building is? Probably by its age in years, right? But what mostly decides a building's lifespan is its concrete skeleton: the columns, beams and floors. Kitchens, wallpaper and plumbing wear out much sooner than the skeleton does. The suit is worn out, but the body inside is still in good shape. There are actually a lot of buildings like that. A whole-building renovation keeps that skeleton and replaces everything inside in one go. Compared with tearing down and rebuilding, it also produces less rubble. In this case, all 161 units will be renovated together, and the building is set to earn an energy-efficiency certification as well. That's a very different range of possibilities from fixing up one unit at a time. Next time you look at an ad for a secondhand condo, check whether, next to the building's age, it tells you how far the renovation went.

Background
The barrier to rebuilding

Soaring construction costs have led to a string of redevelopment and rebuilding projects across Japan being postponed or reconsidered. This year even big projects were affected, with the timing of the Imperial Hotel Tokyo's main building reconstruction becoming undecided. Reviving existing buildings by keeping their structural frames is drawing attention as a realistic option in this situation.

The difficulty of shared ownership

In an ordinary Japanese condominium, each unit has a different owner, so a major renovation or rebuilding of the whole building needs the agreement of many owners. In a whole-building renovation, a developer takes on the entire building first and then sells the units, which makes it easier to renew the interiors and equipment of every unit at the same time.

Grimshaw's new Twelve Apostles visitor center opens in Australia, with a roof that merges into the ground

A new visitor center designed by Grimshaw opened on Oct 16 at the Twelve Apostles, one of Australia's best-known scenic spots. Visits to the site now require a booking.

A new visitor center named Keerray Woorroong opened on October 16 at the Twelve Apostles in the state of Victoria in southeastern Australia. The site, along the Great Ocean Road, is famous for its limestone stacks rising out of the sea.

The building was designed by Grimshaw, an architecture firm founded in the UK, with landscape design by ASPECT Studios. It was designed together with the Eastern Maar Aboriginal Corporation, an Indigenous organization. Rather than competing with the scenery, the building, with a total floor area of about 3,300 m² (36,000 sq ft), is set into the landscape so that it reads as part of the ground, with a rooftop garden and observation deck.

With the opening, visits to the Twelve Apostles now require an advance booking. Entry is free for now, but a fee is scheduled to start in April 2027.

Briefing
  • Of the A$126 million project cost, the federal government paid A$88 million and the Victorian state government A$38 million.
  • Inside are a 230-seat cafe, a kiosk, a gallery, a shop and a rooftop observation deck, the Great Ocean Lookout.
  • Local residents also need to book, but they will be exempt from the fee. The fee amount and which residents qualify have not been decided yet. Revenue from booking fees is to go toward coastal and park programs.

Background
Disappearing architecture

Facilities built near scenic spots or archaeological sites often use techniques that keep the building low-key and blend it into the terrain. Planting the roof or sinking the building into the ground are ways of letting the main attraction, the scenery, take center stage.

Booking systems at tourist sites

At popular tourist sites, moves to introduce booking systems or entry fees to curb crowding and the burden on nature are spreading worldwide. Changing how people get in at the same time as building the facility makes this a case where architecture and operations were planned as a package.
