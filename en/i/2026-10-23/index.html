Shizuoka plans to move its Shimizu city office into a private tower over 60 m (200 ft) tall by January 2032

On Oct 21, Shizuoka City released a draft plan to put its Shimizu government office, which is moving to the area in front of Shimizu Station, inside a mixed-use tower over 60 m (200 ft) tall built by a private developer. It aims to open in January 2032.

Shizuoka City, the capital of Shizuoka Prefecture on Japan's Pacific coast between Tokyo and Nagoya, on Oct 21 released a draft implementation policy and a draft requirements document, which sets out the standards bidders must meet, for its Shimizu government office project. The building houses the Shimizu Ward Office and other city functions. The new office will sit inside a mixed-use building on city-owned land at the east exit of JR Shimizu Station. A private company will build and run the building, and the city will place its office functions inside. The city aims to open the new office in January 2032.

According to media reports, the mixed-use building is expected to have a total floor area of up to about 39,000 m² (420,000 sq ft) and to rise more than 60 m (200 ft), making it a super high-rise under Japanese definitions. The ward office and a child-rearing support center will take the low and middle floors, from the 2nd to the 8th, while the upper floors are expected to hold a hotel, offices, shops and medical facilities.

The project will be procured as design-build (DB), with design and construction handed to a single team, and it is large enough to fall under the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on Government Procurement. The city is collecting comments from private companies considering taking part until November 11 and plans to issue the call for bids in January next year.

Briefing
  • The project site covers about 5,319 m² (57,000 sq ft) in the Sodeshi-cho area of Shimizu Ward. The city will lease it to the private company under a fixed-term land lease, a type of lease that ends on a set date. The mixed-use building is expected to have a total floor area of roughly 30,000 to 39,000 m² (320,000–420,000 sq ft), with at least 12,000 m² (130,000 sq ft) planned for the government office functions.
  • The tender will be a general competitive bid that weighs both price and the content of the proposals. The city plans to issue the call for bids in January, accept expressions of interest until March, and choose the winning bidder in July. A contract will follow after approval by the city council in October.
  • Bids are open to single companies or groups that will handle design, construction, construction supervision and operation of the private facilities. Companies handling design and construction supervision must be certified as building-related construction consultants.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

Hotel rooms stacked on top of a ward office. That's the kind of building Shizuoka City has in mind for the area in front of Shimizu Station. Instead of building its own office, the city will move into floors 2 to 8 of a building put up by a private company. It's the reverse of a convenience store on the ground floor of an apartment building, the kind you see all over Japan. Here, the city government becomes the tenant. Done this way, the city leases out its land and collects ground rent, while the private side keeps the building running by earning money from the hotel and offices upstairs. For a city with tight finances, it's an approach that makes sense. What concerns me, though, is disasters. A government office is the command center for the response after an earthquake or tsunami, right? In a building with members of the public upstairs, how do you separate the emergency power and the evacuation routes? How the requirements document handles that is where the designers will really have to show their skill. The office is set to open in January 2032.

Background
How the move came about

The current Shimizu government office has been aging and is considered to lack sufficient earthquake resistance. After comparing several options for renovating or relocating it, the city decided in 2025 to build a new office in front of JR Shimizu Station, which led to this draft plan.

Sharing a building

Combining a government office and a private building in one structure is being considered in many parts of Japan as a way to make use of city-owned land and hold down construction costs. But it also brings challenges an ordinary government office doesn't face, such as keeping operations running in a disaster and dealing with private tenants moving in and out.

Kajima wins ¥26.45 billion job for a 14-story hotel and retail building linked to Tokyo's Omotesando Station

Tokyo Metro said on Oct 21 that it has chosen Kajima to demolish the old basement and build a new 14-story retail and hotel building directly connected to Omotesando Station, for ¥26.45 billion excluding tax.

Tokyo Metro, the main subway operator in Tokyo, announced on Oct 21 that it has chosen Kajima Corporation as the contractor for the basement demolition and new construction in a joint building development in Kita-Aoyama 3-chome, Minato Ward. According to Nikkan Kensetsu Kogyo Shimbun, a construction industry newspaper, the contract is worth ¥26.45 billion excluding tax, with a construction period of 72 months and completion targeted for fiscal 2032.

The plan is the Kita-Aoyama 3-chome Project, a joint development by Tokyo Metro and Sankei Building. On a site along Aoyama-dori, one of the main avenues in the Aoyama and Omotesando area, the partners will build a mixed-use building with three basement levels and 14 stories above ground, with a total floor area of about 12,800 m² (138,000 sq ft). Retail will occupy the second basement level up to the 2nd floor, and a hotel will take the 3rd to 14th floors. The building will connect directly to Omotesando Station.

The two companies announced the start of the development in April this year. Kume Sekkei is handling the design. Demolition of the above-ground part of the existing building began this month and is scheduled to finish in September 2027.

Briefing
  • According to Nikkan Kensetsu Kogyo Shimbun, the contract was awarded through a general competitive tender covered by the World Trade Organization (WTO) Agreement on Government Procurement, with bids opened on Sept 3. The tender used a comprehensive evaluation method that weighs both price and technical proposals.
  • Omotesando Station is served by Tokyo Metro's Ginza, Hanzomon and Chiyoda lines, and the new building will be a one-minute walk from the station. As of the April announcement, the hotel operator and tenants had not been disclosed.
  • Under the plan the two companies announced in April, new construction is scheduled to start in fiscal 2027. The above-ground demolition that began this month is preparation for that work.

¥26.45 billion. That may look like a big number for a 14-story building. But this job actually means working underground directly above and right next to a station. Omotesando Station is served by three lines: the Ginza, Hanzomon and Chiyoda lines. Without stopping the trains, you tear out the basement of an old building right next door and build a new three-level basement. It takes the kind of care a dentist uses to pull and replace a single tooth without damaging the ones next to it. I think that's why the construction period is 72 months, a full six years. The other interesting part is that everything from the 3rd floor up is a hotel. A subway company is building a place to stay on top of its own station. Walk up the station stairs and you're already at the hotel. For those of you coming to Tokyo on a trip, that's pretty convenient, right? Stations are changing from places you pass through into destinations in their own right. Along Aoyama-dori, what kind of face should this new building show the street, I wonder?

Background
Railways building on stations

Developments in which railway companies build shops and hotels on top of stations or on land directly connected to them are becoming more common across Japan. Rather than relying only on fares, the companies are turning the crowds that stations attract into real estate income. Tokyo Metro also counts development around its stations as one of its business pillars.

Redeveloping Aoyama-dori

Also in Kita-Aoyama 3-chome, new construction began in June on a separate large redevelopment, the Kita-Aoyama 3-chome District Type 1 Urban Redevelopment Project. That is a Japanese scheme in which landowners pool their plots to build a larger complex. Several big construction projects will be running side by side along Aoyama-dori for some time.

Misawa Homes starts site work on ASMACI, a new town on a former university campus in Tagajo, Miyagi

Misawa Homes announced on Oct 20 that it has started land preparation for the ASMACI Tagajo Development Project on the former Tagajo campus of Tohoku Gakuin University in Tagajo, Miyagi Prefecture, in northeastern Japan.

Misawa Homes, a Japanese homebuilder, announced on Oct 20 that it has started land preparation work for the ASMACI Tagajo Development Project on the site of Tohoku Gakuin University's former Tagajo campus in Tagajo, a city next to Sendai in Miyagi Prefecture. The day before, on the 19th, a ceremony to pray for safe construction was held at Mutsu Soshagu, a local Shinto shrine, attended by about 30 people involved in the project.

The plan combines medical care, jobs and housing to create a town where several generations can live comfortably. Along with detached houses and condominiums for sale, commercial, medical and government facilities will be built in phases, with the official opening of the town, known in Japan as machibiraki, targeted for fiscal 2029. Ohmoto Gumi is carrying out the land preparation work.

Tagajo City has acquired part of the site for its own sports and wellness facility project. A key feature of the project is that the private development and the city's facility are being planned and built together.

Briefing
  • Misawa Homes and Tagajo City signed a comprehensive partnership agreement in 2023, and in August that year Misawa Homes signed a contract to buy the land from Tohoku Gakuin, the school corporation that runs the university. The project was originally called the Tohoku Gakuin University Tagajo Campus Site Development Project and has since been renamed the ASMACI Tagajo Development Project.
  • A 2023 announcement said construction would start in October 2025. The land preparation work now under way began about a year after that.
  • Tagajo City is acquiring about 14,500 m² (about 156,000 sq ft) of the site to build its sports and wellness facility. Misawa Homes' development is planned to work together with this city facility.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

An entire university campus is turning into a town. Misawa Homes bought the former Tagajo campus of Tohoku Gakuin University and is developing it. Old university sites are actually great land for building a town. They're large and flat, and roads and water lines are already more or less in place. Isn't there a big campus in your own town that the students have left? Picture houses, shops and a clinic going up little by little on the grounds of a big school, and you'll get the idea. But you can't just clear the land and start building right away. Tagajo is a city with deep history, where the remains of an ancient government office, also called Tagajo, still survive. Here too, reports say work will move forward section by section, depending on how far the archaeological surveys of buried cultural properties have progressed. The start of work came about a year later than originally planned. Layering a new town on top of history takes time. I think that's simply what building a town on this kind of land is like.

Background
What happens to old campuses

With Japan's falling birthrate, universities are consolidating their campuses in city centers, and how to use the suburban campuses they leave behind has become an issue in many places. Because these sites free up large, unbroken tracts of land, they are drawing attention as starting points for housing and mixed-use town development.

Public and private together

More projects are developing private housing and public facilities together on the same site. Compared with a housing-only development, creating a hub where people gather is meant to make the town more attractive and easier to live in.
