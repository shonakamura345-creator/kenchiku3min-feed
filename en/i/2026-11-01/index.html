Toyota Tsusho to move its Tokyo head office from Shinagawa to a new tower by Tokyo Station

Toyota Tsusho announced on October 29 that it will move its Tokyo head office to a redevelopment tower in the Yaesu 1-chome North District, right by Tokyo Station. The move will happen in stages, targeting fiscal 2030, and the Nagoya head office will stay.

Toyota Tsusho, the Toyota group's trading company, announced on October 29 that it will relocate its Tokyo head office by around fiscal 2030 (Japan's fiscal year runs April to March). The office is now in the Shinagawa Front Building in Shinagawa, a business and rail hub in southern Tokyo. Its new home will be the tower going up on the south block of the Yaesu 1-chome North District Type 1 Urban Redevelopment Project in Chuo Ward. Yaesu is the district on the east side of Tokyo Station, and an urban redevelopment project is a Japanese scheme in which landowners pool their plots to build a larger complex. The company plans to move in stages.

The company runs a two-headquarters system with head offices in Nagoya, the largest city in Aichi Prefecture in central Japan, where the Toyota group is based, and in Tokyo. The Nagoya head office will continue to serve as a headquarters. The reasons given for choosing the Tokyo Station area include convenience for employees and the large number of business partners nearby.

The south block tower will rise 44 stories above ground with three basement levels, reaching about 218 m (715 ft). Construction began in November 2024, and completion is scheduled for fiscal 2029. The building will connect directly to JR Tokyo Station, and the plan includes creating waterfront space along the Nihonbashi River as the Metropolitan Expressway, Tokyo's urban toll highway, is moved underground there.

Briefing
  • Toyota Tsusho will move in under a lease with Toyota Real Estate, which is renting part of the tower from Tokyo Tatemono. In other words, the company is leasing the space indirectly through a group real estate company.
  • The Yaesu 1-chome North District redevelopment consists of a south block and a north block, with completion scheduled for fiscal 2029 and fiscal 2032, respectively. Members of the redevelopment association include Tokyo Tatemono and Tokyo Gas Real Estate.

Background
Head offices flock to Yaesu

On the Yaesu side of Tokyo Station, large redevelopments in front of the station keep coming, from Tokyo Midtown Yaesu to TOFROM YAESU and YAESU CORE. In October, ANA decided to move its head office to YAESU CORE in Yaesu 2-chome. New office construction is concentrated in front of the station, and companies are relocating there for the transport access.

Burying the expressway

Work is underway to move the section of the Metropolitan Expressway that runs over the Nihonbashi River around Nihonbashi, Tokyo's historic merchant district, underground. How the riverbanks will be used once the elevated road is gone is being shaped by surrounding redevelopments that build in waterfront plazas and pedestrian spaces. The Yaesu 1-chome North District is one of them.

Fukuoka Airport's new 11-story complex TABISIA, with shops, a hotel and a bus terminal, opens in 2027

Fukuoka International Airport Co. has named its new complex connected to the domestic terminal TABISIA. The 11-story building will house shops, a hotel and a bus terminal, and is set to open around summer 2027.

Fukuoka International Airport Co., which operates Fukuoka Airport in Fukuoka, the largest city on the southern island of Kyushu, announced on October 27 that the complex it is building with a direct connection to the domestic passenger terminal will be called TABISIA. The grand opening is planned for around summer 2027. The name is a coined word combining tabi (Japanese for travel), Asia and fantasia, meaning imagination or fantasy, and the concept is “the traveling airport.”

The 11-story building will have a total floor area of about 43,300 m² (466,000 sq ft), and the total project cost is put at about ¥45 billion. Floors one through four will be retail, and floors five through 11 a hotel. The first floor will have a bus terminal for local and highway buses, and the second floor a stop for the shuttle buses linking the domestic and international terminals.

The retail space will hold about 180 shops, bringing the total to about 280 when combined with those in the existing domestic terminal. The fourth floor will gather food from across Asia, with 44 restaurants planned, including seven from places such as Taiwan and Thailand that are opening in Japan for the first time.

Briefing
  • The Asian food floor on the fourth floor is called ASIYONG. The company says the Taiwanese and Thai brands opening there include restaurants that have been selected for the Michelin Guide's Bib Gourmand.
  • The name was announced on October 27, when the official TABISIA website also began introducing the complex. Details such as the opening date and the shops will be released over time.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

Get off the plane, walk through the arrivals lobby, head to the bus stop. Moving through an airport is usually one straight path like that, right? TABISIA is a plan to slot an 11-story building into the middle of that path. A bus terminal on the first floor, and on the second floor the stop for the shuttle bus to the international terminal. In other words, shops and a hotel are stacked on top of the places you have to pass through to make a connection. It's the same idea Japanese train stations have long used, putting department stores above the ticket gates, now applied to an airport. From a designer's point of view, I think the hard part is sorting out the flow of people. People rushing to catch a bus pass people who want to browse the shops at a leisurely pace, all in the same building. It's the kind of building where a single sign can make a big difference in how easy it is to use. Next time you connect to a bus at an airport, check your watch and see how many minutes it takes to walk from the arrival gate to the bus stop.

Background
Airports becoming towns

Across Japan, airports are being turned from places just for catching flights into places that also serve shopping, overnight stays and transport connections. Fukuoka Airport, only a few subway stops from the city center, is one of the airports with the right conditions to make the airport itself a destination in the city.

Fukuoka keeps renewing

In Fukuoka, beyond the redevelopments around Tenjin and Hakata Station, the city's two main downtown hubs, retail facilities are being renewed one after another, including the outlet rebuild on the former Marinoa City site that we reported on Oct 31. The airport's new complex joins that trend, and 2027 looks likely to be a year when Fukuoka's retail map changes significantly.

Four big Japanese banks cut 10-year fixed mortgage rates for November; MUFG raises to 3.89%

Japan's five major banks announced their November mortgage rates on October 30. Four cut their lowest preferential rate on 10-year fixed loans, while MUFG Bank alone raised it, to 3.89% a year.

Japan's five major banks announced on October 30 the mortgage rates that will apply in November. Four of them, including Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, lowered their best preferential rate on 10-year fixed-rate loans from the previous month. MUFG Bank, by contrast, raised its rate, to 3.89% a year.

The cuts are thought to reflect the recent fall in long-term interest rates. Each bank reviews its fixed mortgage rates monthly, using movements in market long-term rates, such as government bond yields, as a reference.

Even among the major banks, November's decisions were split between raising and cutting. For borrowers, this means the same 10-year fixed loan can carry a different rate depending on which bank they borrow from and when.

Briefing
  • The five major banks that announced November rates are MUFG Bank, Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation, Mizuho Bank, Resona Bank and Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Bank. MUFG Bank was the only one to raise its 10-year fixed rate.
  • MUFG Bank's official website lists a 10-year fixed rate of 3.89% a year for loans taken out in November 2026. Conditions set by the bank apply.
  • The best preferential rate is the lowest rate each bank offers to borrowers who meet its conditions. Actual rates vary depending on screening and loan terms.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

Did you know that mortgage rates change every month? A fixed rate is one that doesn't change after you borrow, but the rate you get depends on the month you borrow, and banks review it every month. So even for the same house, signing just one month earlier or later changes what you pay over 35 years. This time, four big banks cut and just one raised. Here's the thing: seen from the architecture side, this isn't someone else's problem. A housing budget comes down to the price of the building multiplied by the interest rate. Building prices keep rising because of shortages of materials and labor. Add interest rates on top of that, and the house you can build on the same budget gets smaller. Do you trim a few square meters of floor area, or downgrade the fixtures? When someone asks “Can we cut just a little more?” in a design meeting, that question is tied to the bank's rate sheet. Nobody can decide whether next month's number goes up or down. And yet that number is shaping how big our homes can be, isn't it?

Background
Building costs and rates

As we reported on Oct 21, the average price of new condominiums in the Tokyo metropolitan area for April through September topped ¥100 million for the first time. The cost of acquiring a custom-built house, which we covered on Oct 22, has also risen for 14 years in a row. If interest rates stay high while building prices rise, people will start rethinking the size and specifications of their homes.

Fixed vs. variable

Variable rates tend to be influenced by the Bank of Japan's policy rate, while fixed rates are more influenced by long-term market rates. Which is better depends on where rates go from here, and in design and financial planning, the basic rule is to leave room for repayments coming in higher than expected.
