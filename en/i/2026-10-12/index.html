40-story, 160 m condo tower with car elevators planned on former Tokyu Hands site in Osaka's Shinsaibashi

Four developers including Nippon Steel Kowa Real Estate and Kanden Realty & Development plan a high-rise condominium on the former Tokyu Hands site in Shinsaibashi, central Osaka. It will have 138 units and car elevators, with completion set for 2031.

A plan has emerged to build a high-rise condominium tower on the site of the former Tokyu Hands Shinsaibashi store, a branch of the Japanese DIY and lifestyle store chain, in Minamisemba 3-chome, Chuo Ward, Osaka. The Yomiuri Shimbun reported on Oct 9 that four companies, including Nippon Steel Kowa Real Estate and Kanden Realty & Development, will develop it jointly. The tower will have 138 units and is aimed mainly at wealthy buyers, with features such as car elevators that let residents drive their cars right in and ride up. Completion is planned for 2031.

According to a Kansai-region architecture news site, the building will rise 40 stories and about 160 m (525 ft). It sits on a prime corner a few minutes' walk from Shinsaibashi Station on the Osaka Metro, facing the Shinsaibashi-suji shopping arcade, one of Osaka's best-known covered shopping streets. The site was previously home to the Osaka Shinsaibashi 8953 Building, a commercial building that opened in 1999 with 10 floors above ground and two below. Tokyu Hands closed its store there in 2020, the building was demolished in 2025, and the site is now used as a parking lot.

People in the real estate industry point to the site's rarity, noting that only a handful of high-rise condominiums stand within a few minutes' walk of Shinsaibashi Station. However, the construction start date has not yet been confirmed, and details of the plan await further announcements.

Briefing
  • The site is in Minamisemba 3-chome, Chuo Ward, Osaka. The previous building was the Osaka Shinsaibashi 8953 Building, completed in 1999, with 10 floors above ground and two below. Tokyu Hands Shinsaibashi opened in 1999 and closed in 2020
  • The tower is planned at 40 stories and about 160 m (525 ft) tall, according to a Kansai-region architecture news site. The Yomiuri Shimbun reported 138 units and completion planned for 2031
  • The site covers about 1,800 m² (19,000 sq ft) and has been used as a temporary parking lot since demolition (Kansai City Lab)

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

I talked about this on my show too: a tower condominium is a building, but it's also very much a product, one where floor area gets sold directly at a price per tsubo, a Japanese unit of area equal to about 3.3 m² (35.6 sq ft). The Shinsaibashi plan is a textbook example. On a site of about 1,800 m² (19,000 sq ft) facing Shinsaibashi-suji, it puts 40 stories and 138 units. That works out to three or four units per floor. A typical Japanese tower condo has eight to 10 units per floor, so each unit here is quite large. On top of that, there are car elevators, so you ride up to your floor in your own car. In design terms, that means large elevators that can carry heavy cars and a structure with parking built into each floor. All of that costs money, and it comes back in the price per tsubo. A place that used to be Tokyu Hands, open to anyone, becomes a place for 138 households. How do you feel about that rising right at the front of the shopping arcade? Completion is planned for 2031. Five more years until a new tower stands on Shinsaibashi-suji.

Background
Downtown retail turns residential

In Minami, Osaka's southern downtown, hotels and condominiums keep moving into the commercial districts of Shinsaibashi and Namba. Former retail sites are becoming housing because income from commercial tenants is hard to predict as inbound tourism demand fluctuates, while prices for new condominiums in central areas remain high, making housing the more stable business.

Car elevators

High-rise residences with elevators that carry cars up to the residents' floors have precedents in luxury properties overseas, and in Japan they have been adopted in some high-end condominiums in central cities. Because parking spaces are built into each floor, planning the structure and fire compartments is more complex than in a standard tower condominium.

Kintetsu names its Osaka Uehommachi mixed-use building Uehommachi Nexus, bringing back a classic restaurant

On Oct 8, Kintetsu Real Estate named the mixed-use building it is constructing in Uehommachi, Osaka, Uehommachi Nexus. The 12-story building of about 13,000 m² (140,000 sq ft) is designed by Showa Sekkei, built by Asanuma Corporation, and due for completion in February 2027.

Kintetsu Real Estate announced on Oct 8 that the building under construction in Uehommachi 6-chome, Tennoji Ward, Osaka, provisionally called the Uehommachi 6-chome Building, will be named Uehommachi Nexus. The building will rise 12 stories and about 56 m (184 ft), with a total floor area of about 13,000 m² (140,000 sq ft). It is designed by Showa Sekkei and built by Asanuma Corporation, with completion scheduled for February 2027.

The first and second floors will be for restaurants. On the first floor, Hyakuraku, a long-established Chinese restaurant that operated on this spot until 2021, returns under a new brand, For/meLa Hyakuraku. The second floor will house the Meigetsukan yakiniku (Korean-style barbecue) group and a Komeda's Coffee, the third and fourth floors will hold clinics, and the fifth through 12th floors will be offices. There will be rental meeting rooms on the eighth floor and a Kintetsu Real Estate showroom gallery on the 10th.

The site is the former location of Hyakuraku's main restaurant, on Uemachi-suji avenue near Osaka-Uehommachi Station on the Kintetsu railway. Promoted as "a new nexus for Uehommachi," it is planned as a mid-size mixed-use building that brings dining, medical care and offices together under one roof.

Briefing
  • Tenants: For/meLa Hyakuraku on the first floor, the Meigetsukan group and Komeda's Coffee on the second, clinics on the third and fourth, and offices on the fifth through 12th. Rental meeting rooms on the eighth floor and a Kintetsu Real Estate showroom gallery on the 10th
  • Hyakuraku is a long-established Chinese restaurant that operated on the site until 2021. Its return to the site of its main restaurant comes as a revival under a new brand
  • The project was announced at groundbreaking under the provisional name Uehommachi 6-chome Building, and this naming finalizes the facility's name. It stands on Uemachi-suji avenue, close to Kintetsu's Osaka-Uehommachi Station

About 13,000 m² (140,000 sq ft), 12 stories. Tokyo Midtown Nihonbashi, which came up yesterday, is 370,000 m² (about 4 million sq ft), so this is roughly one-thirtieth of that size. But I think it's often buildings of this middle size that do more for a city. The reason is simple: there are so many of them. Japanese cityscapes aren't filled with skyscrapers. They're filled with buildings of around 10 stories. When those get rebuilt one at a time, what goes on the ground floor sets the mood of the street. At Uehommachi Nexus, the ground floor will bring back, in a new form, the Chinese restaurant that stood on that spot until 2021. Instead of a shop vanishing in a rebuild, the building changes and the shop comes home. I think that's the result of both the landowner, Kintetsu Real Estate, and the restaurant treating the memory of the place as an asset. The design is by Showa Sekkei and the construction by Asanuma Corporation, a pairing of a Kansai design firm and a Kansai general contractor. Next time you pass through Uehommachi, take a look at the row of shops on the ground floor. Completion is set for February 2027.

Background
Uehommachi as a place

Osaka-Uehommachi Station is the Osaka-side terminal of the Kintetsu railway, and the area gathers Kintetsu Group facilities such as Uehommachi YUFURA, which includes the Shin Kabukiza theater, and the Kintetsu Department Store Uehommachi in the station building. It is linked underground to Tanimachi 9-chome Station, and it is a calm neighborhood with many schools, hospitals and temples, where condominium development has continued in recent years.

The era of mid-size mixed-use

While rising construction costs make large-scale redevelopment harder to pencil out, more mid-size buildings of 10,000–20,000 m² (110,000–215,000 sq ft) are being built by landowner-type developers on their own land. Combining dining, medical care and offices is becoming a standard formula, spreading vacancy risk while opening the lower floors to the street.

Mitsubishi venture starts building 24 MW, liquid-cooled KIX15 data center near Osaka

MC Digital Realty, a Mitsubishi Corporation joint venture, has started building KIX15, its fifth data center in the Saito area of Ibaraki, Osaka Prefecture. The base-isolated building of about 17,672 m² (190,000 sq ft) is set to open in 2028.

MC Digital Realty (MCDR), a 50-50 joint venture between Mitsubishi Corporation and US-based Digital Realty, announced on Oct 7 that it has begun construction of the KIX15 data center in the Saito area of Ibaraki, Osaka Prefecture. The building has a total floor area of about 17,672 m² (190,000 sq ft), with one basement level and three floors above ground, and uses a base-isolated structure, in which the building sits on bearings that absorb earthquake shaking. It is the fifth building on the company's KIX Campus in Saito.

The facility will have 24 megawatts of power capacity for servers and supports hybrid cooling that combines air and liquid cooling. It is designed to house the high-heat servers used for generative AI, and its main target users are hyperscalers, cloud providers and AI companies. Service is scheduled to begin in the fourth quarter of 2028. According to the Nikkei, it will be the company's 10th data center in Japan.

Once it opens, the company's data centers in Japan will total 10 buildings, with combined server power capacity of about 220 megawatts. Saito is a new town developed in the hills of northern Osaka, and data centers have been clustering there because of its stable ground and the ease of securing power.

  • Cooling is a hybrid of air and liquid cooling. The main target users are hyperscalers, cloud providers and AI companies

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

A total floor area of 17,672 m² (190,000 sq ft), and 24 megawatts of power for servers. A typical household's electricity contract is a few kilowatts, so 24 megawatts means one building using as much power as several thousand homes. Here's the thing about designing a data center: the biggest factor isn't the structure or the look. It's heat. How do you get rid of the heat the servers give off? Until now, the standard approach has been air cooling, with big air-conditioning systems blowing cold air up from under the floor. But servers for generative AI give off heat on a completely different scale, so you need liquid cooling, which circulates coolant directly through the servers. I think KIX15 supports both air and liquid cooling because no one can be sure which will be in greater demand by 2028. The other point is base isolation. What's inside isn't people, it's servers. But if they shake and stop in an earthquake, the services running on them stop too. From base isolation that protects human lives to base isolation that protects data. It feels like we've entered an era where the role of buildings is slowly changing.

Background
Data centers as architecture

Data centers need a lot of power and cooling equipment relative to their floor area, and equipment accounts for much of the construction cost. Sites are chosen for stable ground, proximity to transmission lines and substations, and low flood risk. In the Tokyo area they are clustering in Inzai, Chiba Prefecture, and in the Kansai region in Saito (Ibaraki and Minoh). Base isolation is becoming standard for data centers in Japan.

Liquid cooling as a turning point

GPU servers for generative AI give off several times more heat per rack than conventional servers, and air cooling can no longer keep up. Liquid cooling, which circulates coolant directly to the heat-generating parts of the servers, requires piping and leak protection to be built into the building from the design stage, and it is changing the design requirements for data center buildings.
