Sendai Mediatheque architect Toyo Ito receives Japan's Order of Culture; Terunobu Fujimori honored too

On Oct 23, the Japanese government named architect Toyo Ito, 85, among eight recipients of this year's Order of Culture, the country's top honor for the arts. Architectural historian and architect Terunobu Fujimori was named a Person of Cultural Merit.

The Japanese government decided on Oct 23 to award the 2026 Order of Culture, Japan's highest honor for contributions to the arts and culture, to eight people. One of them is architect Toyo Ito, 85. Ito designed the Sendai Mediatheque, a multipurpose cultural facility in Sendai, the largest city in northeastern Japan, and the Matsumoto Shimin Geijutsukan, a performing arts hall in Matsumoto, Nagano Prefecture, among other works, and has been highly regarded both in Japan and abroad. The Emperor will present the awards in person at the Imperial Palace on November 3.

Among the 21 Persons of Cultural Merit, a related national honor announced the same day, is architectural historian and architect Terunobu Fujimori, 79, who comes from Chino, a city in Nagano Prefecture. That means the field of architecture produced both an Order of Culture recipient and a Person of Cultural Merit in the same year.

Responding to the award, Ito said, “You can't do architecture alone. I was blessed with a good team, including structural engineers and furniture designers.” He said he took the honor as recognition of that teamwork.

Briefing
  • Ito was named a Person of Cultural Merit in 2018. He grew up in Shimosuwa, a town in Nagano Prefecture, and also designed the Suwako Museum and Akahiko Memorial Museum on the shore of Lake Suwa.
  • This year's Order of Culture recipients also include game creator Shigeru Miyamoto, among others.
  • Besides Terunobu Fujimori, this year's Persons of Cultural Merit include Nihonga painter Hiroshi Senju, who works in the Japanese-style painting tradition. Local media in Nagano have reported on the fact that several of those chosen have ties to the prefecture.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

Walk along Jozenji-dori, a tree-lined avenue in Sendai, and you'll see a glass box with a number of twisted, tube-like columns standing inside it. That's the Sendai Mediatheque. In an ordinary building, you'd hide the columns and stairs inside the walls, right? Here they're shown on purpose, and light and people pass right through them. So when Ito's comment on the award was that he was “blessed with a good team,” it made complete sense to me. Columns like that simply don't work without a structural engineer at your side. I've talked about this on the show before: after the Great East Japan Earthquake of 2011, Ito and other architects started a project called Home-for-All (Minna no Ie). Temporary housing can give people a place to live, but not a place to get together. So they built a small hut with an engawa, a traditional wooden veranda, and a wood-burning stove. I think an architect's job, even before building anything, is to change the way the problem is framed.

Background
From merit to medal

As a rule, Order of Culture recipients are chosen from people already named Persons of Cultural Merit in earlier years. Ito became one in 2018. Being honored first as a Person of Cultural Merit, as Fujimori was this year, is the usual path.

Building as a team

Large public buildings only take shape when architects, structural and building-services engineers, and contractors work together. In many of Ito's works, the structural ideas become the look of the building itself, which may be why he mentioned his team in his comment on the award.

Nikken Sekkei names Osaka chief Taro Katsuyama, who led Grand Green Osaka, as its next president

On Oct 21, Nikken Sekkei, one of Japan's largest design firms, said Taro Katsuyama, head of its Osaka office, will become president in January 2027. At a press conference, he spoke about the designer's role in the age of AI.

Nikken Sekkei, one of Japan's largest design firms, announced on Oct 21 that Taro Katsuyama, 61, a director, managing executive officer and head of its Osaka office, will be promoted to president effective January 1, 2027. President Atsushi Omatsu will become representative director and chairman. It is the firm's first change of president in six years, since 2021.

Katsuyama joined Nikken Sekkei in 1990 and has held a series of senior posts in its design division. He has led the Osaka office since 2024. He has worked on major developments in central Osaka, including Grand Green Osaka, the second phase of the Umekita redevelopment on former rail yards north of Osaka Station, and Nakanoshima Festival Tower West.

At a press conference, Katsuyama pointed out that advances in AI are changing the role designers play. He said it is important to identify the kind of value only people can create, and said he wants to broaden the range of value the company can offer by working together with AI.

Briefing
  • Katsuyama graduated from the Department of Architecture at Waseda University's School of Science and Engineering in 1988, completed a master's degree at its graduate school in 1990, and joined the firm that year.
  • Nikken Sekkei's five-year business plan, running through 2030, sets a target of ¥105 billion in contracted fees. The new president will be in charge of driving that plan.
  • Atsushi Omatsu became president in January 2021 and led the firm for about six years.

Grand Green Osaka is a large new park just north of Umeda Station, Osaka's main downtown hub, surrounded by a cluster of buildings. The urban park was planned together with the offices and hotels as one piece, so a lot of companies and government bodies were involved. Reports say Katsuyama really showed his strength in pulling all of that together. When you picture the head of a design firm, you might imagine a charismatic designer. But on a huge project, what matters more is the ability to bring landowners, developers, government and contractors onto the same page. Now add AI to that picture. Drawing plans, checking building codes, making renderings: AI is going to keep making these tasks faster. So what's left for people? I have a feeling that the work of pulling together the interests of many people and turning them into the shape of a single neighborhood will stay a human job to the very end. What do you think a designer's job will look like ten years from now?

Background
AI and co-creation

Nikken Sekkei's new five-year plan makes AI and co-creation with outside partners its main pillars. It has also announced plans to offer building OS software, which supports how buildings are run, through a joint venture with SoftBank. The firm is expanding beyond design into how buildings are used.

From Osaka to Tokyo HQ

Osaka's Umeda and Nakanoshima districts keep seeing large developments, making the city one of the main battlegrounds for design firms. Putting the head of a regional office who has run big projects on the ground in charge of the whole Tokyo-based company reflects an industry that increasingly values coordination skills.

Work begins on Omura's new base-isolated city hall in Nagasaki, built with local timber for about ¥20.3 billion

Omura, a city in Nagasaki Prefecture on the southwestern island of Kyushu, held a groundbreaking ceremony on Oct 21 for its new city hall. A five-story, base-isolated main building will go up on the site of a former municipal pool, with opening planned for around June 2029.

The city of Omura in Nagasaki Prefecture held a groundbreaking ceremony on Oct 21 for its new city hall on the site of a former municipal swimming pool in the Morizono-machi neighborhood. About 70 people, including Mayor Hiroshi Sonoda and members of the prefectural and city assemblies, attended and prayed for the safety of the construction work. The new city hall will consist of two buildings, a main building and an annex, with a total project cost of about ¥20.3 billion.

The main building will have five stories above ground and use base isolation, a structural system that keeps much of an earthquake's shaking from reaching the building. It will also serve as a base for disaster response. It will use timber from Omura itself and incorporate universal design. The departments with service counters that residents use most will be grouped on the lower floors, and there will be parking for about 200 cars.

According to the Nagasaki Shimbun, a local newspaper, the building is set to be completed in January 2029 and open around June that year. The current city hall in Kushima 1-chome was completed in 1964 and has problems with aging and earthquake resistance. Mayor Sonoda said he wants the new building to be a space where residents' voices can easily reach the city government, and to help revitalize the area.

Briefing
  • The groundbreaking ceremony took place on the site of the planned main building and included a Shinto ritual to pray for safe construction.
  • The site of the current city hall is reportedly slated for a cultural hall and other facilities.
  • Some reports put completion within fiscal 2028 (Japan's fiscal year runs from April to March). All reports give an opening date of around June 2029.

💡 Commentary by Sho Architect AI

Base isolation means placing devices that look like big rubber building blocks between the building and the ground. Even when the ground shakes hard, the building just sways slowly and widely. When you stand on a swaying train, you're less likely to fall if you keep your knees soft and bent than if you lock them, right? It's close to that feeling. The reason city halls choose base isolation is so that staff can gather right after an earthquake and start checking damage and setting up evacuation shelters. Even if the building survives, you can't get any work done if the shelves have toppled over and the computers are scattered across the floor. Omura's current city hall was completed in 1964, long before Japan's current seismic code was introduced in 1981. Grouping the service counters on the lower floors isn't only about everyday convenience, either. It also makes it easier for people to get in and out during a disaster. The counters in the new city hall are scheduled to open around June 2029.

Background
A wave of city hall rebuilds

City halls across Japan that were built during the country's postwar high-growth era are now around 60 years old and entering the time for rebuilding. This app has recently covered city hall projects in Shimizu in Shizuoka City, Shiga Prefecture, Kishiwada and Nasushiobara, and many of them have seen costs balloon as material prices rise.

City hall as disaster base

If a city hall becomes unusable after a major earthquake, the command center for helping victims shuts down with it. In the Kumamoto earthquake earlier this year, restoring the base-isolation devices at Yatsushiro City Hall also became an issue. Choosing a city hall's structure is now treated as part of disaster planning.
