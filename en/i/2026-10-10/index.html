Update: Fukuoka's Tenjin Parco block redevelopment plan puts total cost at ¥165.8 billion

A group led by the Shintencho shopping arcade has drawn up the project plan for a redevelopment in Tenjin, downtown Fukuoka. Total cost is ¥165.8 billion, with construction set to start in March 2027 and finish in December 2031.

A project plan has been drawn up for the Tenjin 2-chome South Block Station-Front East District Type 1 Urban Redevelopment Project in Tenjin, the main downtown and shopping district of Fukuoka, the largest city on the southern island of Kyushu. The construction trade paper Kensetsu Tsushin Shimbun reported on Oct 9 that the plan was put together by the Shintencho Shopping Arcade Commercial Cooperative, the Shintencho Shopping Arcade Corporation, Nishi-Nippon Railroad (Nishitetsu), Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking, and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation. The site covers about 1.5 hectares (3.7 acres), and the total project cost is ¥165.802 billion. Construction is to start in March 2027 and finish in December 2031, with the project period running through March 2034. Takenaka Corporation is the specified agent contractor, the builder that takes on part of the project on the landowners' behalf.

As we reported on Sep 14, the City of Fukuoka had announced a redevelopment building with four basement levels and 22 stories above ground, about 141,000 m² (1.52 million sq ft) of total floor area, a height of about 110 m (360 ft), and a rough construction cost of about ¥123 billion. The new project plan gives the total floor area as 141,471 m² (1.52 million sq ft). The total cost breaks down into ¥117.511 billion for the building itself, ¥5.119 billion for surveys, design and planning, ¥8.619 billion for the Meruhen underground passage, and ¥9.847 billion for land preparation, among other items. Retail will occupy the second basement level through the seventh floor, with offices and a high-end hotel on the eighth floor and above.

At the September session of the city council, Fukuoka reported that the design and construction cost for the East District had risen from about ¥100 billion in its fiscal 2025 estimate to about ¥123 billion. The neighboring West District, on the Shintencho side, has a site of about 5,900 m² (64,000 sq ft) and about 88,000 m² (950,000 sq ft) of total floor area. Demolition there is expected to begin around spring 2027, with completion sometime in the 2030s.

Briefing
  • The developers are the Shintencho Shopping Arcade Commercial Cooperative, the Shintencho Shopping Arcade Corporation, Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking, Nishi-Nippon Railroad and Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation. Parco and J.Front Urban Development are also listed among the developers on the Parco block side
  • In the redevelopment building, retail will fill the second basement level through the seventh floor, with offices and a high-end hotel on the eighth floor and above
  • Tenjin Big Bang is an urban redevelopment policy the City of Fukuoka launched in 2015. The aviation law caps building heights at 67 m (220 ft) because of the nearby airport, but on the west side of Meiji-dori avenue the cap has been relaxed to about 115 m (380 ft)
  • In the West District, on the Shintencho side, demolition is expected to begin around spring 2027, with completion sometime in the 2030s

A redevelopment this size is carried out by a cooperative, a railway company and trust banks acting together as one developer. The shop owners of a shopping arcade are shouldering a ¥165.8 billion plan as co-developers. If the shopping street in your own town had to make this call, how would you think it through?

Background
Specified agent contractors

A specified agent contractor (tokutei gyomu daikosha) is a private company that an urban redevelopment association hands a bundle of work to, including construction and part of the financing. The role usually goes to a general contractor, which takes on some of the association's project risk in exchange for building the project. Takenaka Corporation is involved in several redevelopments in the Tenjin area.

The Tenjin Big Bang deadline

Fukuoka's Tenjin Big Bang uses the deregulation available in a National Strategic Special Zone to encourage rebuilding. To qualify for exceptions on floor area ratio (FAR) and height limits, buildings must be completed by a deadline, originally the end of 2024 and now extended to the end of 2026. Because the Station-Front East District is due to be finished in 2031, its exceptions are expected to be handled under a separate framework.

Pritzker-winning architect Riken Yamamoto runs for mayor of Yokohama on a right to housing

Riken Yamamoto, 81, the architect who won the 2024 Pritzker Prize, is running as an independent for mayor of Yokohama. His platform centers on a right to decent housing and his Local Community Area concept. Voting is on Oct 18.

Architect Riken Yamamoto, 81, has entered the Yokohama mayoral election as an independent. The official campaign began on Oct 4. Yamamoto designed the Yokosuka Museum of Art and Future University Hakodate, among other works, and in 2024 he won the Pritzker Prize, often called the Nobel Prize of architecture. The election was triggered by the previous mayor's resignation in August over a power harassment controversy involving city employees. Seven candidates are running, including the former mayor. Voting takes place on Oct 18.

According to the local paper Town News, Yamamoto said at a press conference after filing his candidacy that he would guarantee the right to live in safe, comfortable housing. He proposes treating each neighborhood association, the residents' groups found in neighborhoods across Japan, as a Local Community Area. His platform includes neighborhood association salons that bring together support for children and older people, government service counters and cafes, along with a stronger public transit network, more tourism revenue and upgrades to aging municipal housing. Nikkei Xtech also reported that he is calling for protection of the right to live comfortably.

The Local Community Area (chiiki shakai ken) is an idea Yamamoto has promoted for many years as an architect. It rethinks the one-house-one-family model, in which each household lives closed off in its own home, and proposes groups of a few hundred people sharing housing, work and everyday services. With this campaign, an architect is putting his own theory forward as city policy.

Briefing
  • Yamamoto was born in 1945 and founded his office, Riken Yamamoto & Field Shop, in 1973. His major works include the Yokosuka Museum of Art, Saitama Prefectural University and Future University Hakodate. He has also taught as a professor at the graduate school of Yokohama National University
  • His pledges also include seismic assessments and ground surveys for every home in the city, a network of minibuses and mini-taxis that residents can ride for free, and a goal of growing tourism revenue from about ¥500 billion to ¥1 trillion (according to his official website and the election bulletin)
  • The election follows the previous mayor's announcement on Aug 27 that he would resign. The campaign officially opened on Oct 4, early voting runs Oct 5 to 17, and voting day is Oct 18

An architect is running for mayor. He's one of seven candidates, and I want to take that simply as a fact. What I want to talk about here isn't whether he wins or loses. It's that his platform is, at its core, architectural theory. The Local Community Area is a concept Yamamoto has been laying out in books and exhibitions since the 2010s. In short, it says: take homes that are closed around a single family, open them up a little, and let a few hundred neighbors support one another. Think of a house with a small shop or workspace next to the front door, or an apartment building whose ground floor works as everyone's living room. That's a design question, and at the same time a policy question about where neighborhood associations and government counters should go. Normally an architect can only test an idea like this inside the buildings he designs. This candidacy is an attempt to test it from the side of the city's own systems. What possibilities, and what worries, do you see in a city government deciding how our homes should open up? Voting in the Yokohama mayoral election is on Oct 18.

Background
The Local Community Area

A housing concept proposed by Riken Yamamoto. It questions the one-house-one-family model that postwar Japanese housing policy took for granted. Instead, it takes a group of about 500 people as the basic unit, opens part of each home for a small shop, a workspace or a shared living room, and lets residents share everyday services. Yamamoto presented it in books and exhibitions in the 2010s, and it sparked debate in the architecture world.

Japan and the Pritzker Prize

The Pritzker Prize is an international architecture award founded in 1979. Japanese winners include Kenzo Tange, Fumihiko Maki, Tadao Ando, Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa, Toyo Ito, Shigeru Ban and Arata Isozaki. With Riken Yamamoto in 2024, Japan has produced nine winners (counting Sejima and Nishizawa as one).

Update: Design exam for Japan's top architect license is tomorrow, the first with code books allowed

The 2026 design drafting exam for the first-class kenchikushi license, Japan's top architect license, takes place tomorrow, Oct 11. This year's brief is a hotel, the exam lasts 6 hours 30 minutes, and candidates may bring code books for the first time. Results come out Dec 23.

The 2026 design drafting exam for the first-class kenchikushi license, Japan's top architect license, which covers buildings of any size, will be held nationwide tomorrow, Sunday, Oct 11. It is the second stage of the licensing exam, after a written test, and candidates must produce a full building design on paper within the time limit. As we reported on Sep 24, this year's brief is a hotel. The exam runs from 11:00 to 17:30, a total of 6 hours 30 minutes, preceded by an explanation of the rules starting at 10:35. Results are scheduled for Dec 23.

This is the first year candidates can bring code books, compilations of building laws and regulations, into the drafting exam. According to the exam guidelines from the Japan Architectural Education and Information Center (JAEIC), the body that runs the exam, candidates may use up to two code books, and may add the supplements and errata sheets that come with them. As in the building regulations section of the written exam, there are rules on handwritten notes and index tabs, and books will be checked on the day.

This year 29,094 people took the written exam and 7,619 passed, a pass rate of 26.2%, sharply up from 16.5% the year before. According to Sogo Shikaku Gakuin, a major exam-prep school, that is the highest pass rate since the exam took its current format in 2009. Besides this year's written-exam passers, the drafting exam also includes candidates who passed the written exam within the past two years.

Briefing
  • The drafting exam schedule: 10:35 to 11:00 is an explanation of the rules (25 minutes), and 11:00 to 17:30 is the exam itself (6 hours 30 minutes)
  • The pass mark for the written exam was 94 points in total. People in their 20s made up 64.7% of those who passed (according to an analysis by Sogo Shikaku Gakuin)
  • Those who pass the written exam can also take the drafting exam in the following two years. Each written-exam pass allows up to three attempts at the drafting exam
  • Drafting exam results are scheduled for Wednesday, Dec 23. Those who pass can go on to register for their kenchikushi license

Background
Why the written pass rate jumped

The 2026 written exam had a pass rate of 26.2%, up 9.7 percentage points from the year before. The pass mark was adjusted upward from the initial guideline of roughly 90 points to 94, yet 7,619 people still passed, the most in recent years. With more candidates sitting the drafting exam, which is graded on a relative basis, some expect competition for the top grade to be tougher than usual.

How the drafting exam is graded

The design drafting exam is graded on four ranks, I to IV, and only Rank I passes. Fatal mistakes such as a building code violation or a missing required room put a candidate in Rank IV, a fail no matter how good the drawings are. One thing to watch this year is whether allowing code books changes how code-related mistakes are treated.
